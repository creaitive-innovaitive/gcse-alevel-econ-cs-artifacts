Writing Evaluation That Scores

A framework for when "it depends" isn't a cop-out but an argument

What evaluation actually is
Evaluation is not "point for, point against, and a sentence that says 'overall it's complicated.'" It is a judgement — you must decide which side is stronger, and say under what conditions your judgement changes. The examiner is paying for the weighing-up, not for having two lists.

The CIT Framework

Use this for every evaluative paragraph, and again for your conclusion:

StepWhat to write
ConditionState the factor your judgement depends on: magnitude, time period, elasticity, type of economy/market, who is affected.
If"If [condition] is true/large/short-run..." — spell out one scenario.
Then"...then [outcome] because [mechanism]." Link it back to the specific claim in the question.

Repeat CIT for the opposite condition, then finish with a judgement sentence that says which condition is more likely / more significant / more relevant to this context — and why.

Five reusable evaluation levers

When you don't know what to evaluate on, pick one of these — they work for almost any AS Econ question:

LeverUse it to ask...
MagnitudeIs the effect big enough to matter, or small and easily offset?
TimeDoes the short-run effect differ from the long-run effect?
ElasticityDoes the outcome depend on PED/PES/YED/XED being high or low?
Type/contextDoes it differ between developed/developing, large/small, competitive/monopoly, open/closed economies?
Trade-offDoes fixing one problem (e.g. inflation) create another (e.g. unemployment)? Who gains, who loses?

Sentence starters — steal these

Building the two sides
"This is only true if..."
"However, this assumes that... which may not hold because..."
"The significance of this depends on the size of..."
"In the short run X, but in the long run Y, because..."
"This is more likely to apply to [type of economy/market] than to [other type], because..."
Building the final judgement (use the question's own wording)
"On balance, [claim from the question] is more likely to hold where..., because... This is more significant than [the other factor] because..."
"Therefore, [claim] is only true to a limited extent — it depends primarily on..."

Common evaluation failures — and the fix

What you're doingWhy it failsFix
"It depends on many factors."No factor named, no mechanism, no judgement. Examiner reads this as a stall.Name ONE factor, explain the mechanism, then judge.
Repeating your explain paragraph with "but overall" tacked on.No new reasoning — this is a summary, not evaluation.Every evaluation sentence must introduce a condition not yet mentioned.
Two separate paragraphs, "for" then "against," never compared.Examiner needs to see you weigh them against each other, not just list both.End with one sentence directly comparing which is stronger and why.
Evaluating in general terms: "the economy," "consumers," "the government."The condition is true of any market, so it earns little.Name the group and the good in the condition: "If demand among long-term smokers is inelastic..."
Judgement doesn't use the question's exact wording.Reads as generic; examiner can't award "answers the question" credit.Your final sentence should reuse the key noun/verb from the question stem.
Make the condition specific
Generic "This depends on the elasticity of demand."
Applied "This depends on whether demand for cigarettes among long-term smokers is price inelastic; if it is, a 20% tax raises revenue but barely reduces consumption, because addicted smokers keep buying."

Practice

Question: "Evaluate the view that raising the minimum wage always increases unemployment."

Write one CIT paragraph for "yes, unemployment rises" and one for "no, it might not," then a judgement sentence.
