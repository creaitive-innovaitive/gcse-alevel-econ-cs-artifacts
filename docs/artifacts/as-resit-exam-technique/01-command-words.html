Command Words Decoder

AS Economics 9708 Paper 2 — read the verb before you read anything else

The rule
Before you write a single word, underline the command word. It tells you the ceiling of your answer — how far you're allowed to go and what the examiner is paying you for. Most Paper 2 marks are lost not because the economics is wrong, but because the student answered the wrong type of question for the command word given.
Command wordWhat it actually wantsWhat it does NOT want
Define / StateA precise, textbook-accurate statement. One or two sentences. No example needed unless asked.Do not explain, do not give examples, do not evaluate. Padding here wastes time and gains nothing.
IdentifyName it. From the data/context given if it says "from the data."No explanation required — if you explain, you're not wrong, just slow.
Describe / OutlineState the points or main features of a topic, or the pattern shown in data. A list of accurate points is enough.Not the mechanism (that's Explain) and not a judgement.
ExplainA chain of reasoning: point → mechanism → consequence. Show HOW/WHY step by step, using economic terms and (where possible) a diagram.Not a list of points with no linking. Not an opinion. Not "it depends" — that's for evaluate.
AnalyseExplain in depth, showing chains of consequences (X causes Y causes Z), often with a diagram, applied to the context/data given.Not a judgement. Don't conclude "so it's a good/bad thing" — that's evaluation, save it.
CalculateWork out the number from the data given. Show your working, state the formula if there is one, and give the unit.No explanation needed unless the question separately asks for it.
CompareIdentify and comment on both the similarities and the differences between the things named, with reference to specific figures or features.Not two separate descriptions with no link drawn between them.
DemonstrateShow how something works, or give an example of it, usually with a diagram or a worked case.Not a bare definition with no illustration.
Give / JustifyGive: state an answer, from the source or from recall. Justify: support your answer with evidence or a reasoned argument for why it is correct.Justify is not just restating the claim — it needs a reason.
CommentAn informed opinion, briefly reasoned. Lighter-weight than Evaluate, but still needs a reason, not just an assertion.Not a full CIT evaluation unless the mark allocation calls for it.
DiscussBoth sides developed with roughly equal weight, then a reasoned judgement.Not one-sided. Not a list of "for and against" with no linking sentences or final judgement.
ConsiderReview the information or argument given and respond to it directly, usually leading into a judgement. In practice on Paper 2/4 this behaves like Discuss or Evaluate: review both sides, then decide.Not a one-sided summary of the given information with nothing added.
Evaluate / Assess / To what extent / Do you agreeA judgement, backed by criteria, that weighs the argument — not just presents both sides. Must directly answer the question asked, using the exact wording of the question in your conclusion.Not new content. Not a summary of what you already said. Not "it depends" with nothing after it.

Full official list (2026–2028 syllabus): Analyse, Assess, Calculate, Comment, Compare, Consider, Define, Demonstrate, Describe, Discuss, Evaluate, Explain, Give, Identify, Justify, Outline, State.

Why this matters for you specifically
Your Paper 1 economics is solid. The examiner's report keeps saying NAQ (Not Answering the Question) and Too vague. Both usually happen because you answered the topic the question was about, not the command the question gave. "Explain" got treated like "Discuss." "Evaluate" got treated like "Explain twice." Fix the verb, fix the mark.

The 10-second check before you write

  1. Circle the command word.
  2. Circle every noun/scope word — country, government, firm, market, consumers — and any word like ALL / EVERY / ALWAYS / ONLY.
  3. Ask: "Is this asking me to explain a mechanism, weigh up a judgement, or just state a fact?"
  4. Match your answer structure to that — not to what you feel like writing about.

Practice: circle the command word and say what it demands

Question stemCommand wordWhat structure is needed?
Explain why a fall in interest rates might increase consumer spending.
Discuss whether governments should always subsidise merit goods.
To what extent does devaluation always improve a country's trade balance?
Analyse the impact of a minimum wage on unemployment in a competitive labour market.
Whatever the command word: apply it
The verb sets how far you go, but every level of answer needs the economics applied to the case the question names. Most 12-mark questions use Discuss, Evaluate, Assess or To what extent, so see the 12-Mark Structure (page 7) for the shape of the answer and Application (page 5) for how to make it specific.
