NAQ & "Too Vague" Fixer

Spot it, name it, rewrite it — practice with real examiner-style comments

Two different diseases, two different cures
NAQ = you answered a question that wasn't asked. Usually: wrong command word, ignored a scope word (ALL/ONLY), or answered about the general topic instead of the specific claim.

Too vague = you answered the right question, but didn't say anything precise enough to be marked. Usually: no named mechanism, no diagram reference, no numbers/context from the data, generic terms like "the economy" or "this could affect things." Test: could the sentence appear unchanged in an answer about a different market? If yes, it is generic. See Application (page 5).

Diagnose each answer below

For each, write NAQ, Vague, or OK, then say why in one sentence.

QuestionStudent answerDiagnosis + why
Explain how a rise in the price of oil could affect a country's rate of inflation."Oil prices going up would be bad for the economy and cause problems for everyone."
Discuss whether ALL governments should use fiscal policy to reduce unemployment."Fiscal policy means the government spends more or taxes less. This can create jobs by increasing aggregate demand, so government spending is a good way to reduce unemployment."
Analyse the effect of a fall in the exchange rate on a country's export industry."A weaker exchange rate makes exports cheaper in foreign currency terms, which is likely to increase the volume of exports demanded (assuming demand is price elastic), raising export revenue and output in the export sector, which may also reduce cyclical unemployment there."
Evaluate whether subsidies are the best way for a government to increase consumption of merit goods."Subsidies could work well or might not work, it depends on the situation and how the government decides to use them."
