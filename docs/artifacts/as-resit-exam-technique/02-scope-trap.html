The Scope Trap: "ALL" vs "AN"

The single biggest reason you're getting zero marks on questions you actually understand

What's happening
The examiner writes: "Discuss whether ALL economies would benefit from free trade."
You write: "An economy that specialises in exports may benefit from free trade because..."

This is correct economics. It scores zero, because it doesn't answer the question that was actually asked. The question asked about every economy, without exception. You answered about one hypothetical economy that happens to fit your example. These are different questions.

Why the examiner does this

Words like ALL, EVERY, ALWAYS, ONLY, NEVER in a question are a deliberate trap. They are testing whether you can spot that a general claim is almost never universally true in economics — and whether you can explain why it breaks down for some economies, not just describe a case where it works.

Scope word in questionWhat it forces you to do
ALL economiesYou must consider economies that are different from each other — e.g. developed vs developing, large vs small, resource-rich vs resource-poor, open vs closed — and show the claim does NOT hold for at least one type.
ALWAYSFind the condition under which it fails. "Always" is disproved by one credible exception.
ONLYYou must show other things could also cause it, or the stated cause isn't the only route to the outcome.
The scope word is part of the context
ALL economies means ALL economies, not AN or THE economy. This is application as well as technique: the question names who or what it is about, and your analysis has to be about that. In the June 2024 examiner report, an answer on a developing economy was marked down because that economy was mentioned only "in passing". See Application (page 5).

The fix: the "Not-For-Everyone" checklist

Whenever you see ALL / ALWAYS / EVERY / ONLY, before you write your answer, fill this in on your plan:

Plan before you write
1. Type of economy/case where the claim DOES hold:
2. Type of economy/case where the claim does NOT hold, and why:
3. What characteristic explains the difference (size, openness, development, market structure, elasticity, time period)?

Worked example

Question: "Discuss whether ALL economies benefit from a depreciation of their currency."

NAQ Weak answer: "A depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports more expensive, so a country's export revenue may rise and this could improve the current account. This shows depreciation benefits an economy."
Problem: never engages with "ALL." Treats one mechanism as if it applies everywhere. No exception considered. No judgement on the word ALL.
Strong Better answer: "Whether ALL economies benefit depends on characteristics the question assumes are constant but aren't. For an economy with elastic demand for exports and imports (the Marshall-Lerner condition holding), depreciation can improve the trade balance... However, for an economy that is highly import-dependent for raw materials or food (many small, resource-poor developing economies), demand for imports is price inelastic, so depreciation raises the import bill faster than export revenue rises, worsening the trade balance in the short run (J-curve) or permanently if the condition fails. Therefore ALL economies do not benefit equally — the outcome depends on the price elasticity of demand for traded goods, which varies systematically between economy types."

Practice: rewrite these to actually answer the ALL/ALWAYS version

QuestionYour answer must contrast at least two types of...
Discuss whether ALL firms benefit from operating in a monopoly.
Evaluate whether governments should ALWAYS intervene to correct negative externalities.
