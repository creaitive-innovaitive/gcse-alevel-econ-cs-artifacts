The 8-Mark Question: Explain ... and Consider

AS Economics 9708 Paper 2, part (a) — from whole answer, to paragraph, to sentence

What the question is, every time
Every part (a) in the 2023–2025 papers has the same shape: Explain [concept or reasons] and consider [a judgement question]. Examples: "Explain what is meant by protectionism and consider the effectiveness of using tariffs"; "Explain three causes of unemployment and consider which cause is likely to be most significant"; "Explain the difference between a public good and a private good and consider the extent to which a beach could be described as a public good." Two instructions, two jobs, and the second one is worth marks.

How the 8 marks are awarded

Part (a) is point-marked, not levels-marked. The mark scheme splits it the same way every time.

MarksWhat earns themWhere in your answer
3 × AO1 KnowledgeAccurate definitions, the key concept explained, and an example (or a correct labelled diagram where the question asks for one)."Explain" part
3 × AO2 AnalysisA chain of reasoning: this causes that, which leads to the next thing, so the outcome is X."Explain" part
2 × AO3 EvaluationOne mark for a valid evaluative point that answers the "consider" wording. One mark reserved for a justified conclusion."Consider" part
The cost of skipping "consider"
The 2025 examiner report says the same thing about similar 6-markers: candidates who skip the evaluation "limited themselves to a maximum" of the analysis marks. In an 8-marker that is a cap of 6 out of 8. The examiner reports for 2024 and 2025 also say most candidates gave little or no evaluation on part (a) even though the question asked for it. Two to four lines of "consider" is the cheapest marks on the paper.

Aim for about 10–12 minutes: 1 minute to underline both instructions, about 7 minutes on Explain, and about 3 minutes on Consider. Write roughly 70% explain, 30% consider.

Running example used on every level: "Explain the difference between a public good and a private good and consider the extent to which a beach could be described as a public good." (9708/22, June 2025 Q3a)

Level 1 — Answer structure

Two blocks, in the order the question gives them. Do not write an opener or a long introduction.

BlockWhat it containsRough size
1. ExplainDefine the concept or concepts the question names, give an example, then analyse: a developed chain of reasoning, using a labelled diagram if the question asks for one or the topic needs one. If the question says "two" or "three" reasons, give exactly that number, each as a short chain.6–8 lines
2. ConsiderAnswer the "consider" wording directly: how far, whether always, which is most significant, or what the limitation is. State the condition that decides it, then a justified conclusion in the question's own words.3–4 lines
Five "consider" wordings seen in recent papers
WordingWhat your Consider block must do
"...consider the extent to which [X applies / will work]"Say how far, and name the condition that decides it. Conclude with a degree: "largely", "partly", "only if...".
"...consider whether [X] will always be true"Give one case where it is not true, then conclude: usually / not always, because...
"...consider which of these is most significant / likely"Compare your two or three reasons directly and pick one, with a reason for ranking it first.
"...consider a limitation of this approach"One developed limitation, then a one-line verdict on how serious it is.
"...consider the effectiveness of [policy]"One thing that makes it work, one that limits it, and a verdict.

Level 2 — Paragraph structure

Block 1: the Explain paragraph (6 of the 8 marks)

PartJob
DefineDefine each concept the question names in its own sentence, using the correct technical terms. If the question asks for a difference, define both and state the contrast.
ExampleOne accurate example for each concept. Check it: roads, education and health are not public goods just because a government provides them.
ChainTwo or three linked steps showing why it matters: cause → mechanism → consequence. Refer to your diagram if you drew one.
Explain A private good is excludable and rivalrous: a seller can stop non-payers using it and one person's consumption leaves less for others, for example a restaurant meal. A public good is non-excludable and non-rivalrous: nobody can be prevented from using it and one person's use does not reduce another's, for example national defence. Because non-payers cannot be excluded from a public good, consumers have an incentive to free ride, so firms may be unable to charge a price and make a profit. A market is therefore likely to under-provide public goods or not provide them at all, which is why they are usually funded by government.

Block 2: the Consider paragraph (2 of the 8 marks)

PartJob
TurnStart with a word that signals the shift: "However", "To what extent depends on...". This tells the examiner the AO3 marks are starting.
CompareOne or two sentences weighing the case for against the case against, or one reason against another. A comparison, not a summary of what you have just written.
ConcludeOne sentence answering the question in its own words, with the reason. This is the reserved mark.
Consider A beach could be described as a public good to some extent. If it is open access and uncrowded, nobody can be excluded and one visitor's use does not reduce another's, so it is non-excludable and non-rivalrous. However, on a crowded holiday beach one person's space reduces the space left for others, so it becomes rivalrous, and an operator can fence it and charge entry, making it excludable. A beach is therefore best described as a quasi-public good: it behaves like a public good only when it is uncrowded and unfenced.

Level 3 — Writing the sentences

Explain sentences: define → mechanism → consequence

The test for analysis: could you insert "because" or "which leads to" and keep going? If the sentence just states a fact or a result, it is not yet analysis. Keep definitions short, because the marks are for accuracy, not length.

Sentence stems for the Explain block
"[Term] is [defining features], for example [specific example]."
"The difference is that [A] is [feature] whereas [B] is [feature]."
"[Event / policy] causes [variable] to [rise / fall] because [reason]."
"This leads to [next effect], which means [outcome for the market or economy]."
Level of sentenceExample
Too vague"Public goods are things the government provides for everyone, like roads."
Knowledge"A public good is non-excludable and non-rivalrous, for example national defence."
Analysis"Because non-payers cannot be excluded, consumers may free ride, so firms cannot charge a price and are unlikely to provide the good."

Consider sentences: condition → comparison → verdict

The examiner report for June 2025 is direct about what does not count: summative statements, and strings of statements that start "it depends upon". Evaluation is the comparison of strengths and weaknesses, leading to a conclusion that answers the question set. A sentence earns evaluation credit if it names a specific condition and says what happens either side of it.

Sentence stems for the Consider block
"This depends on [specific condition], because if [condition], then [outcome], but if not, then [different outcome]."
"However, [limitation], because [mechanism], so [effect on the answer]."
"[Reason A] is more significant than [reason B] because [criterion: size, speed, likelihood, who is affected]."
"Overall, [answer to the question in its own words], because [main reason]."
Level of sentenceExample
Not evaluation"So a beach is a public good because everyone can use it."
Too vague"Overall it depends on the beach and other factors."
Evaluation"A beach is only non-rivalrous while it is uncrowded, so on a busy holiday it is better described as a quasi-public good."

Common ways to lose marks on part (a)

ProblemFix
Ignoring "consider"Underline it in the question. If your last line is still explaining, you have not started the AO3 marks.
Wrong number of points"Explain three causes" means three. A fourth earns nothing, and two caps the knowledge marks.
Confusing similar conceptsExaminers have flagged public goods vs government-provided private goods, and the terms of trade vs the balance of trade. Define precisely.
No diagram when asked"With the help of a diagram" means the diagram has marks attached. Label both axes, curves and equilibrium points, and refer to it in the text.
Explaining a different version of the topicAnswer the exact question. In 2025 many answers described consumption of merit goods when the question asked about production.
Conclusion that restates"In conclusion a beach can be a public good" repeats the stem. Say how far and why.

Practice

Question: "Explain what is meant by protectionism and consider the effectiveness of using tariffs as a method of protection."

1. Define protectionism and tariffs in two sentences, with one example.

2. Write the chain showing how a tariff protects domestic firms (3 steps).

3. Write the Consider block: one limit on effectiveness, then a justified conclusion.
