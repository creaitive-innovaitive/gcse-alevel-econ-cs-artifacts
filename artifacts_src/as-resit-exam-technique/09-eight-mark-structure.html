The 8-Mark Question: Three Levels of Structure

AS Economics 9708 Paper 2 — from whole answer, to paragraph, to sentence

How to use this page
An 8-marker is a shorter, tighter version of the 12-marker. It rewards knowledge, application and a developed chain of analysis, with little or no room for extended evaluation unless the command word asks for it. Aim for about 10–12 minutes, with 1 minute of planning. For the longer question, see the 12-Mark Structure (page 7).

Running example used on every level: "Explain why a government might impose a maximum price on rental housing."

Level 1 — Answer structure

Two developed paragraphs, or one long one plus a short second. Do not write a list of four thin points: depth earns the marks, not breadth. Check the exact mark split in the mark scheme you are working from.

PartWhat it containsRough size
Definition (if needed)One sentence defining the key term the question turns on, in your own words, only if it earns a knowledge mark. Skip it if the term is obvious.1 sentence
1. Main reason / effectThe strongest point, as a developed chain of reasoning, with a labelled diagram if the topic allows one.6–8 lines
2. Second reason / effectA different point, not a restatement of the first. Also a chain, though it may be shorter.4–6 lines
Judgement (only if asked)If the command word is Assess, Evaluate or Discuss, add one short CIT sentence (Condition → If → Then). If the command word is Explain or Analyse, do not spend time here.2–3 lines
Match the command word
Explain / Analyse: all the marks are in the chain of reasoning, so add a second developed point instead of a judgement.
Assess / Evaluate / Discuss: keep both points shorter, and finish with a brief weighing sentence.

Level 2 — Paragraph structure

Analysis paragraph (used for each main point)

PartJob
PointOne sentence stating the reason, using the wording of the question.
MechanismThe economic reason it happens. Name the concept (supply, elasticity, externality, AD/AS) and refer to your diagram. Apply it now: use the case's own words, numbers and stakeholders (see Application, page 5).
Consequence chainTwo or three linked steps: this causes that, which causes the next thing. Apply to the context.
LinkOne sentence tying the result back to the question.
Point 1 A government might impose a maximum price on rental housing to make homes affordable for low-income households. If the maximum rent is set below the equilibrium rent, rents are held down from P1 to Pmax, so households on low incomes could afford to rent where previously they could not. Because housing is a necessity with a large share of low-income budgets, the policy may free up income for food and transport, which is likely to raise their standard of living. The maximum price is therefore a way of correcting what the government sees as an unfair market outcome.
Point 2 A government might also use it to protect tenants from sudden rent rises in a market where supply is slow to respond. Housing supply is price inelastic in the short run because building takes years, so a rise in demand from migration or population growth pushes rents up sharply. A maximum price caps the rise, which may give tenants stability and reduce the risk of being priced out of the area.

Judgement sentence (only if the command word asks for it)

PartJob
CITOne condition (lever) → If it holds → Then this outcome, because [mechanism].
Judgement The policy is only likely to help tenants if the maximum rent does not reduce supply too far. If landlords respond by leaving the market, the quantity supplied falls below the quantity demanded, so there may be a shortage that leaves some low-income households with no housing at all.

Level 3 — Writing the sentences

Analytical sentences: cause → mechanism → consequence

An analytical sentence shows how something happens. The test: could you insert "because" or "which leads to" and keep going? If the sentence only states a fact or a result, it is not yet analysis, and in an 8-marker you cannot afford many of those.

Sentence stems for analysis
"[Policy / event] causes [variable] to [rise / fall] because [reason]."
"This leads to [next effect], which in turn [final effect]."
"This matters in [context] because [specific feature of the market or economy]."
"Therefore, [link to the question wording]."
Level of sentenceExample
Too vague"A maximum price makes rent cheaper so poor people can afford it."
Analysis"Setting the maximum rent below equilibrium lowers the price tenants pay, so quantity demanded rises and more low-income households could afford housing, though the lower price may also reduce the quantity landlords supply."

Common ways to lose marks on an 8-marker

ProblemFix
Four short pointsCut to two and develop each into a chain. Lists cap at the knowledge marks.
No applicationName the market, group or country from the stem or extract in the mechanism sentence.
Long evaluation on an Explain questionSpend the time on a second analysis point instead.
Diagram drawn but not usedRefer to it in the text ("the shift from S1 to S2 raises price from P1 to P2") or it earns nothing.

Practice

Question: "Explain why a government might impose a tax on sugary drinks."

1. Write the point sentence for your main reason.

2. Write the mechanism and consequence chain (3 steps, at least one applied detail).

3. Write a second, different point in 3–4 lines.
