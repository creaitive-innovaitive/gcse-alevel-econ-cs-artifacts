Economic Rent and Transfer Earnings

Two ideas that look hard and are not. Simple English, one story, pictures you build step by step, and every past paper question that used the ideas, with the mark schemes.

The two ideas in two sentences

Transfer earnings are the smallest pay a worker needs to stay in their job. If they get less, they transfer (move) to another job.

Economic rent is all the extra pay above that smallest pay. The worker would stay without it, but the market gives it to them anyway.

Total pay = transfer earnings + economic rent

"Rent" here has nothing to do with paying for a house or flat. It is an old economics word for pay that is more than you need to get the factor to work.

1 · Start with one person

Mia the teacher

Mia earns $30,000 a year as a teacher. She loves teaching. She says: "I would stay for $25,000, but not for less."

  • Transfer earnings = $25,000. This is the smallest pay that keeps her in the job.
  • Economic rent = $30,000 − $25,000 = $5,000. This is the extra she gets.

Leo the fashion model

Leo earns $500,000 a year. The best other job he could do is teacher at $100,000. So if a model agency paid him less than $100,000, he would move to teaching.

  • Transfer earnings = $100,000. The pay in his next best job.
  • Economic rent = $500,000 − $100,000 = $400,000.
Which number is the transfer earnings?Use the number the question gives you. If it gives the pay in the next best job, that is the transfer earnings (Leo). If it says the smallest pay the person would accept to stay, that is the transfer earnings (Mia). Do not use the pay in a better job the person does not want.

2 · Now a whole market: why one wage makes rent

Every worker has their own smallest pay. A firm pays everyone the same wage. So almost every worker gets more than they needed. That extra is economic rent.

WorkerSmallest pay needed (transfer earnings)Wage paidExtra (economic rent)
Ana$6$10$4
Ben$7$10$3
Chi$8$10$2
Dev$9$10$1
Eli$10$10$0
Total$40$50$10

Eli is the last worker the firm hires. Eli gets exactly the smallest pay, so no rent. Everyone before Eli gets some rent. The supply curve is just this list of "smallest pay" numbers drawn as a line.

3 · The picture

On a labour market diagram, the supply curve shows the smallest pay for each worker. The wage line is the pay everyone gets. Then:

Area under the supply curve = transfer earnings

"Under" the curve, from zero up to the number of workers hired. This is the smallest pay of all workers added together.

Area above the supply curve, below the wage = economic rent

The triangle between the supply curve and the wage line. This is the extra pay.

4 · How steep is the supply curve?

This is the idea examiners love. Elasticity of supply means: how easy is it to find more workers of this kind?

Easy to find more workers (elastic supply)

Example: street cleaners. Almost anyone can do the job after a short time. The supply curve is flat. Everyone would work for about the same pay, so nearly all of the pay is transfer earnings. Very little rent.

Hard to find more workers (inelastic supply)

Example: a famous tennis player. Only a few people in the world can play like this. The supply curve is steep. Their next best job pays much less, so nearly all of the pay is economic rent.

Supply curveShapeTransfer earningsEconomic rent
Perfectly elasticHorizontal lineAll of the payZero
ElasticFlatMost of the paySmall
InelasticSteepSmallMost of the pay
Perfectly inelasticVertical lineZeroAll of the pay
Why vertical means all rentA vertical supply curve means the number of workers cannot change, whatever the pay. So nobody will move to another job when pay falls. The smallest pay is zero, so all pay is extra.

5 · What changes rent and transfer earnings?

  • Demand rises (people pay more to watch the sport): the wage rises. With steep supply the wage rises a lot, so rent rises a lot. With flat supply the wage hardly changes.
  • Time: in the short run supply is steep (it takes time to train new workers). In the long run it is flatter, so some rent in the short run becomes transfer earnings in the long run.
  • A minimum wage or union wage above equilibrium: workers who keep their jobs get a higher wage, so their rent goes up. Some other workers lose their jobs.
  • Supply becomes more elastic (the supply curve gets flatter through the same point): transfer earnings rise and economic rent falls.

6 · Not just workers

The idea works for any factor of production. For enterprise, the transfer earnings are normal profit: the smallest profit that keeps the entrepreneur in the business. Any profit above that, supernormal profit, is economic rent.

7 · Three mistakes to avoid

Mistake 1Thinking "rent" means rent for a house. It does not.
Mistake 2Mixing up transfer earnings with transfer payments. Transfer payments are money from the government (such as unemployment benefit). Different topic.
Mistake 3Saying rent is only for rich people. A teacher can earn rent too, if they get more than they need to stay.
Mistake 4Reading the areas the wrong way round. Remember: under the supply curve = transfer; above it (below the wage) = rent.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. The numbers are simple on purpose, to show the idea.

The famous tennis player (and the street cleaner)

A top tennis player could earn about the same as an ordinary person in the next best job, if tennis did not exist. Because very few people have the talent, supply is steep. Millions of fans watch, so demand is strong. The wage is very high and almost all of it is economic rent.

A street cleaner is different. Many people could do the job after a short training. Supply is flat, so even if demand rises the wage hardly changes, and most of the pay is transfer earnings.

Exam use: this is exactly what the Oct/Nov 2022 Paper 4 question asked. Use the two types of worker side by side.

A teacher who loves the job

A teacher earns $30,000 and would stay for $25,000. The $5,000 gap is economic rent. It may be small, but it is still rent.

Exam use: shows that rent is not only for rich people. Matches the Feb/March 2025 multiple choice.

A fashion model

A model earns $500,000. As a teacher the model could earn $100,000. So $100,000 is transfer earnings and $400,000 is economic rent.

Exam use: a calculation question from Oct/Nov 2022. Subtract, then check the two parts add to the total pay.

A company boss

The boss of a large bank may earn hundreds of times more than the average worker. Very few people can run a large bank, so supply is steep and most of the pay is likely to be rent. Demand is high because a good boss may add a lot to profits.

Exam use: a good example for "why do wages differ?" questions (see the indirect questions on the Past papers tab).

Farm workers in a busy season

In the short run it is hard to find skilled workers, so supply is steep and some of the pay is rent. In the long run more people can learn the skills, supply becomes flatter and the rent turns into transfer earnings.

Exam use: a short run and long run question from Oct/Nov 2023.

A union wage

A union wins a wage above the market wage. Workers who keep their jobs now get more extra pay. Their economic rent rises. But fewer workers are needed, so some people lose jobs.

Exam use: appears in the June 2026 and Oct/Nov 2023 multiple choice. Combine it with the effect on employment for analysis marks.

Oct/Nov 2022 · 9708/41 · Q4(b) 13 marks Distinguish between economic rent and transfer earnings and, with the help of diagrams, discuss why famous sports people receive much higher pay than street cleaners.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Define both terms and show the difference on a labour market diagram. Transfer earnings are the smallest pay to keep a worker in the job. Economic rent is any pay above that.
  • Link to elasticity of supply. Famous sports people have special talents, so supply is limited and very inelastic. If demand to watch is strong, pay is very high. Most of their pay is economic rent because their skill cannot easily be used in another job.
  • Street cleaners: low skills and short training, so supply is high and elastic. Even if demand rises a lot, pay is unlikely to rise much. Most of their pay is transfer earnings.
  • Evaluation (other factors). Sports pay could fall if fans lose interest in one player or in the sport. Street cleaner pay could rise through a trade union or a change in government policy on public sector pay.
  • A conclusion is needed for the top level.

KANE

Transfer earnings are the smallest payment needed to keep a worker in their present job, while economic rent is any payment above that amount. On a labour market diagram the area under the supply curve up to the wage shows transfer earnings, and the area above the supply curve and below the wage shows economic rent. The flatter the supply curve, the larger the share of pay that is transfer earnings.

Famous sports people are likely to be paid much more than street cleaners because their supply is very inelastic. Very few people can play tennis at the top level and the talent cannot be copied quickly, so even a large rise in the wage is unlikely to bring many more players. When millions of fans want to watch, demand is high and the wage is pushed up, so most of a top player's pay may be economic rent. Street cleaners have a highly elastic supply because the job needs few skills and little training, so a rise in demand may raise their wage only a little and most of their pay is likely to be transfer earnings.

However, elasticity of supply is not the only reason. A sports person's pay could fall if fans lose interest in the player or the sport, and street cleaner pay could rise if they join a trade union or if the government raises public sector pay. Overall, the difference in supply elasticity is likely to be the main reason for the large pay gap, but demand and policy may change how big it is.

May/June 2025 · 31 and 33 · Q14 and 32 · Q14 1 mark each Three labour markets. Which two areas are economic rent? (32 asks: which two areas are transfer earnings?)

Papers 31 and 33: options (A) 1 and 3, (B) 1 and 4, (C) 2 and 3, (D) 2 and 4. Paper 32: same options, with the answer (C) 2 and 3.

Rent: 1 and 4. Transfer earnings: 2 and 3. In the first diagram, area 1 is above the supply curve (rent) and area 2 is under it (transfer). In the second diagram the supply is horizontal (perfectly elastic), so area 3 is all transfer earnings. In the third diagram supply is vertical (perfectly inelastic), so area 4 is all economic rent.
Oct/Nov 2023 · 31 · Q12 1 mark The government sets a minimum wage W1. Which area is the increase in economic rent of the workers who keep their jobs?

Options: (A) X, (B) Y, (C) X + Y, (D) X + Y + Z.

Answer: A (X). Before the minimum wage, the N2 workers who keep their jobs already received rent Y (area between We and the supply curve). At W1 they get rent X + Y. The increase is X. Area Z is rent lost by the workers who lose their jobs.
Oct/Nov 2023 · 33 · Q11 1 mark Short run and long run supply of labour. Which area changes from economic rent in the short run to transfer earnings in the long run?

Options: (A) X, (B) X + Y, (C) Y, (D) Y + Z.

Answer: C (Y). The short run supply curve is steeper, so rent is the area to its left (X + Y). The long run supply curve is flatter, so rent is only X. Area Y is rent in the short run but becomes transfer earnings in the long run. Z is transfer earnings in both.
Oct/Nov 2024 · 33 · Q14 1 mark Supply of labour changes from S1 to S2. What happens to transfer earnings and economic rent?

Options: (A) fall, falls · (B) fall, rises · (C) rise, rises · (D) rise, falls.

Answer: D. S2 is flatter (more elastic) through the same point. The area under the supply curve becomes bigger (transfer earnings rise). The area above it becomes smaller (economic rent falls).
June 2026 · 34 · Q11 1 mark A union negotiates wage WT. What is the economic rent?

Options: (A) T + V + X, (B) V + U, (C) V + X, (D) X + Y + Z. (The diagram also shows areas U, Y and Z, which are not needed for the answer.)

Answer: C. At WT employment falls to N2. Rent is the area above the supply curve and below WT, up to N2: V (below the old wage W1) plus X (between W1 and WT). T is under the supply curve, so it is transfer earnings.

Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/42 · Q4 25 marks The pay of the bosses (CEOs) of the six biggest US banks was 250 to 360 times the average pay of the staff. All six CEOs were male. Discuss the extent to which economic theory can account for these variations in wages.
Mark scheme (summary)Expected: supply and demand for labour, MRP theory, elasticity of supply and demand, discrimination, and the differing objectives of the firm. L4 (18–25): accurate discussion of differences in supply and demand and of elasticities, including MRP, with reference to discrimination, linked to the context, with a conclusion (maximum 21 marks without a conclusion). L3 (14–17): analysis of supply and demand with some reference to elasticities; maximum L3 if there is no MRP. L2 (10–13): description of differences in supply and demand. L1 (1–9): some knowledge, irrelevant points and errors.
Where economic rent fitsA bank CEO has a very inelastic supply: very few people can run a large bank. Demand is high because the CEO's MRP is large. So the pay is very high, and most of it is economic rent, because the CEO's next best job pays much less. This gives strong, specific analysis and links the diagram to the wage gap in the question. For evaluation, discrimination (all six CEOs were male) and the firm's own objectives may also matter, as the mark scheme says.

May/June 2021 · 9708/41 and 43 · Q5 25 marks "Wage differentials can be explained by economic theory. They are a sign of the power of a firm to exploit its workforce and are unjust." Do you agree with this view?
Mark scheme (summary)Discuss whether the theory explains wage differentials and comment on the statement. Say what "differential" means (same job, different people, or different jobs). In imperfect competition (monopsony) wages may be lower than in perfect competition, but differentials may also come from differences in supply and the shape of the MRP curve, public and private sector pay and political choices. L4 (18–25): thorough analysis of wage determination, clear discussion of "differential", and all three parts commented on (explained by theory, due to firm power, unjust). L3 (14–17): competent theory, two parts. L2 (10–13): descriptive, one part. L1 (1–9): some knowledge, irrelevant.
Where economic rent fitsPart 1 of the statement: differentials can be explained by differences in elasticity of supply. Rent explains why a surgeon or a sports star earns far more than the smallest pay they need. That is not exploitation: it comes from scarce talent. Part 2: a monopsony pays below the competitive wage, which reduces the workers' rent.
Oct/Nov 2022 · 9708/42 · Q4 25 marks Discuss the extent to which marginal revenue product theory can be used to explain the determination of wages.
Mark scheme (summary)Expected: demand for labour from MRP (MPP × MR), elasticity of demand and supply and the effect on wages, monopsony and minimum wage law, and questioning the assumptions (MPP, MR, a perfect labour market). L4 (18–25): analysis using MRP, comments on elasticities, imperfections linked to wage levels, assumptions questioned; a conclusion for 22–25. L3 (14–17): MRP analysis plus either elasticity comments or imperfections. L2 (10–13): limited supply and demand explanation. L1 (1–9): basic facts, errors.
Where economic rent fitsMRP only explains demand. To explain the full wage you need supply as well. Oct/Nov 2024 · 9708/42 · Q1(c) 6 marks Using the information and labour market theory, analyse why wages of migrant workers in high-tech industries are likely to be higher than wages of migrant workers in hospitality.
Mark scheme (summary, 1 mark per point)Demand: the MPP of educated workers is likely to be higher; the value of their output is greater; demand for them is higher, leading to higher wages. Some graduates become entrepreneurs and earn high profits. Prejudice against less educated workers may be reflected in their pay. Supply: when immigration falls, the wage gap for the low paid may narrow and widen for the educated.
Where economic rent fitsA strong extra point: high-tech skills take years to learn, so supply is inelastic and a large part of the pay may be economic rent. Hospitality jobs can be learnt quickly, so supply is elastic and most of the pay is transfer earnings.

Top tips

  • Always say "minimum pay to keep the worker in the job" for transfer earnings. Use these words in your definition.
  • Economic rent = payment above transfer earnings. Add "over and above what is needed to keep the factor in its present use".
  • Draw the diagram, label the axes, supply, demand, wage and shade the two areas. Say what each area is.
  • Under the supply curve = transfer. Above it, below the wage = rent.
  • Link to elasticity every time. Steep supply = more rent. Flat supply = more transfer earnings.
  • Use two examples side by side: a famous sports person and a street cleaner, or a CEO and a shop worker.
  • Evaluate: demand could fall, unions or the government could change pay, time makes supply flatter, discrimination may affect pay.
  • In calculations, check that transfer earnings + economic rent = total pay.
