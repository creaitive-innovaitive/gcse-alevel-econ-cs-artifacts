Buying Panic at the Bottom

A rules-based way to look at sharp, fear-driven falls: five signals that all have to agree before the setup counts, and an exit decided before the trade starts. Educational and historical only; nothing here is advice or a recommendation.

The idea in one minute

Most strategies follow a trend. This one does the opposite. It is a mean-reversion setup: when a price has fallen so far and so fast that the move looks statistically extreme, a bounce becomes more likely than a further collapse. It only looks at the daily chart (one candle, or one point, per day), and it fires only a handful of times a year.

Top tipThe edge in this setup is patience, not cleverness. Three or four signals out of five feels like a bottom but is not the setup. Only five out of five counts.

The sequence

1. Wait

Watch the daily chart. Do nothing while fewer than five conditions are true.

2. Check all five

RSI, Bollinger Band, the 30% dip, the volume spike and the support level must all be true at once.

3. Enter

When the fifth condition lands, the setup is complete. It will feel uncomfortable because price has just fallen hard.

4. Exit by plan

Place a sell limit order 30% above the entry price straight after entering, so no decision is made under pressure.

    The exit

    Straight after entering, a sell limit order is placed 30% above the entry price. A limit order is an instruction to the broker to sell automatically once the price reaches a level you set, so nothing has to be decided while watching the screen.

    Top tipA limit order either fills or it does not. Setting the target in advance removes the temptation to hold on for more, or to sell early out of fear.
    What the rules do not includeThe post describes an entry and a take-profit but no stop-loss. A setup that looks complete can still keep falling, so in any historical study it is worth asking what a failed trade would have cost. The Signals tool has stop-loss settings for exactly that kind of test.

    Does it actually work? Reading a backtest

    A backtest runs the rules against historical prices to see what would have happened, without hindsight. You can run your own on the Growth Capital Group Backtester. The post reports this result for the setup on SOL-USD over a six-month window.

      Multiple choice

      Historical illustrations only, in general terms. Nothing here is a recommendation.

      In history

      March 2020

      US shares fell roughly a third in about a month as the pandemic took hold, with record trading volumes. It is a textbook example of the kind of fear-driven fall this setup describes.

      Exercise: pull the daily data for one index and check how many of the five conditions were true, and on which days.

      Crypto sell-offs

      Large crypto assets have had several days where prices fell tens of percent within weeks on heavy volume, which is why the post tests it on SOL-USD.

      Exercise: ask whether a fall that big always found support, or whether some kept falling after all five lined up.

      2008 and 2022

      Broad market indices have had long falls as well as sharp ones. A setup that waits for a sharp, fast fall would have looked very different in a slow decline, where prices can keep falling without a single panic day.

      Use it for: asking when a mean-reversion rule would and would not have applied.

      Trading volume in a sell-off

      In many sharp falls, trading volume jumps to several times its usual level as holders sell in a hurry. The volume condition tries to capture that.

      Use it for: understanding why volume is one of the five conditions.

      Exam-style questions

      Write your answer first, then open the mark points. These are educational questions about how the rules work, not about what anyone should do.

      Explain 3 marks Explain why this setup waits for all five conditions rather than any one of them.

      1. A single condition (such as RSI below 30) is common and often appears long before a fall ends.

      2. Requiring all five at once makes the setup rare, so it only flags the most extreme falls.

      3. Loosening the rule makes it fire constantly, mostly on days that were not the bottom.

      Calculate 3 marks A study of 5 trades has gross profit of £3,000 and gross loss of £1,000. Calculate the profit factor and the win rate if 3 trades won.

      1. Profit factor = gross profit ÷ gross loss.

      2. £3,000 ÷ £1,000 = 3.0.

      3. Win rate = 3 ÷ 5 = 60%.

      Evaluate 4 marks The post reports a 100% win rate and 0% maximum drawdown for the setup. Evaluate how much these figures show.

      1. The figures come from a single closed trade, so the sample is tiny.

      2. A 100% win rate on one trade says little about how the rules behave across many falls.

      3. The rules include no stop-loss, so a failed trade could have been much worse than the sample shows.

      4. A longer test across several assets and periods would be needed before drawing conclusions.

      State 2 marks State what a sell limit order is and why the setup uses one.

      1. An instruction to sell automatically once the price reaches a level set in advance.

      2. It means the exit is decided before the trade, so nothing has to be decided under pressure.

      Top tips

      • All five or nothing. Three or four out of five is not the setup.
      • RSI below 20, not 30. The strategy waits for the rarer reading.
      • Quote the sample size. A headline statistic from one trade proves little.
      • Say what is missing. The rules in the post have no stop-loss.
      • Keep it historical. Describe what the rules would have flagged, not what to do next.
